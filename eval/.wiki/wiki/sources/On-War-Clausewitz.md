# On War — Carl von Clausewitz (1832)

> War is not an autonomous art but the continuation of state policy by violent means, forever degraded from theory by chance, uncertainty, and friction.

## Author's thesis
Clausewitz argues that war is fundamentally an act of organized violence whose purpose is to compel the enemy to submit to one's will, but that this violence is never an end in itself — it is a political instrument that serves the policy of the state. Because war is subordinate to policy, its aims and intensity are set from outside the battlefield, and pure "absolute" war exists only on paper. In the real world, the gap between plan and execution is filled by *friction*: the countless small frictions, chance events, physical dangers, and uncertainties that make even the simplest thing difficult. Strategy, for Clausewitz, is the art of using engagements to serve the object of the war, working always under incomplete information ("the fog"). The result is a theory that refuses geometric certainty and centers on the interplay of policy, moral forces, and the genius of the commander.

## Key concepts introduced or developed
- [[War-as-Continuation-of-Policy]] — war is a political instrument, not an act divorced from statecraft.
- [[Friction]] — the accumulation of minor difficulties that separates real war from war on paper.
- [[Fog-of-War]] — the province of uncertainty in which three-fourths of the factors are hidden.
- [[Strategy]] — the employment of engagements to achieve the object of the war.
- [[Military-Genius]] — the harmony of intellect and courage required to act amid danger and doubt.

## Extracted claims
- War is defined as an act of violence intended to compel the opponent to fulfil one's will. ^[raw/books/clausewitz-on-war/book.md:1086-1087]
- War is not merely a political act but a real political instrument — a continuation of political intercourse carried out by other means, with policy as the object and war as the means. ^[raw/books/clausewitz-on-war/book.md:1781-1791]
- Friction is the only conception that in a general way distinguishes real war from war on paper; the military machine looks simple but is composed of individuals each generating friction in all directions. ^[raw/books/clausewitz-on-war/book.md:3535-3540]
- War is the province of uncertainty: three-fourths of the things on which action is based lie hidden in clouds of great uncertainty. ^[raw/books/clausewitz-on-war/book.md:2609-2611]
- Strategy is the employment of the battle to gain the end of the war; it forms the plan of the war and links the series of acts leading to the final decision. ^[raw/books/clausewitz-on-war/book.md:6355-6361]

## Contrasted with
- [[Art-of-War-Sun-Tzu]] — Sun Tzu prizes winning without fighting and subduing the enemy by deception and foreknowledge, whereas Clausewitz centers the decisive battle and the destruction of enemy forces.
- [[Meditations-Marcus-Aurelius]] — Marcus seeks inner tranquility and withdrawal from the passions of conflict; Clausewitz treats organized violence as a rational instrument of the state.
