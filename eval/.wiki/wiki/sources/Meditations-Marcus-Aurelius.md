# Meditations — Marcus Aurelius (c. AD 180, Casaubon translation)

> The private notebook of a Roman emperor practising Stoic philosophy on himself:
> how to keep an even mind, act with virtue, and accept what is not in one's power.

## Bibliographic

- **Author(s):** Marcus Aurelius (tr. Meric Casaubon)
- **Year:** written c. AD 170–180
- **Source file:** [[../../raw/books/marcus-aurelius-meditations/book.md]]

## Author's thesis

Disturbance comes not from things but from our opinions about them; so govern the
opinion. Live according to reason and nature, do your duty, accept fate, and
remember that only the fleeting present is ever truly yours. This is Stoicism
turned into daily self-discipline.

## Key concepts introduced or developed

- [[Stoicism]] — the philosophy the whole book practises
- [[Dichotomy-of-Control]] — what is and is not in our power
- [[Virtue]] — living according to nature and reason as its own end
