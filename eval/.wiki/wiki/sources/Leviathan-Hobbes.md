# Leviathan — Thomas Hobbes (1651)

> Without a common power to hold all men in awe, life collapses into a war of every man against every man; escape lies only in surrendering natural right by covenant to an absolute sovereign.

## Author's thesis
Hobbes argues that human beings are by nature roughly equal in strength and cunning, so that in a condition without common power each is a standing threat to every other. From this equality of hope and scarcity flows a "war of every man against every man," in which there is no industry, no culture, no society, and life is "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short." Reason discovers Laws of Nature commanding men to seek peace, but these are ineffectual without enforcement. The only remedy is for the multitude to covenant with one another, giving up their right of self-government to one man or assembly — the sovereign — who bears their common Person. This "Mortall God," the Leviathan, holds the pooled power needed to keep peace at home and provide defence abroad; his authority must be undivided and near-absolute, because a divided or resisted sovereign is a return to war.

## Key concepts introduced or developed
- [[State-of-Nature]] — the condition of mankind without common power, a war of all against all.
- [[Social-Contract]] — the mutual covenant by which each man lays down his right to the sovereign.
- [[Sovereignty]] — the undivided authority that bears the person of the multitude and enforces peace.
- [[Right-of-Nature]] — the natural liberty to use one's own power for self-preservation.
- [[Law-of-Nature]] — precepts of reason commanding the pursuit of peace and self-preservation.
- [[Security]] — protection of life as the end that justifies and limits political obedience.

## Extracted claims
- Nature has made men so equal in faculties of body and mind that the weakest can kill the strongest, leaving no man secure by his own strength alone. ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:3924-3924]
- Without a common power to keep them in awe, men are in a condition of war "of every man, against every man" — war being not just fighting but the known disposition to it over time. ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:4015-4026]
- In this condition there is no industry, culture, navigation, arts, or society — only "continuall feare, and danger of violent death," and the life of man is "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short." ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:4033-4044]
- The Right of Nature is the liberty each man has to use his own power for the preservation of his life; while it endures and every man has a right to everything, there can be no security to anyone. ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:4128-4175]
- The fundamental Law of Nature, found by reason, commands "To seek Peace, and follow it," while the right of nature permits, when peace fails, to defend ourselves by all means we can. ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:4180-4188]
- Peace requires conferring all power on one man or assembly by a covenant of every man with every man — "I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe, to this Man" — generating the "great LEVIATHAN," that "Mortall God" to whom we owe peace and defence. ^[raw/books/hobbes-leviathan/book.md:5661-5686]

## Contrasted with
- [[On-Liberty-Mill]] — Mill limits state power by the harm principle and defends the individual against authority, where Hobbes concentrates near-absolute authority in the sovereign.
- [[The-Republic-Plato]] — Plato grounds the just state in the wisdom of philosopher-kings and the soul's order, not in a self-interested covenant born of fear and scarcity.
- [[Walden-Thoreau]] — Thoreau prizes self-sufficiency and civil resistance to the state, the opposite of Hobbes's demand for unconditional submission for the sake of security.
