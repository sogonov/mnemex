# On the Origin of Species — Charles Darwin (1859)

> Life's diversity is not separately created but descends, with modification, from common ancestors, sculpted by a blind, incessant natural selection acting through the struggle for existence.

## Author's thesis
Darwin argues that species are not fixed, independently created forms but the modified descendants of earlier species. Because far more organisms are born than can survive, individuals compete in a universal "struggle for existence"; any heritable variation that gives even a slight advantage tends to be preserved, while less-favored variants perish. Darwin names this preservation of favorable variations "natural selection," analogizing it to the breeder's artificial selection but calling nature's power immeasurably superior. Accumulated over vast time, selection acting on divergent lineages explains adaptation, the branching "Tree of Life," and the origin of new species from common ancestors.

## Key concepts introduced or developed
- [[Natural-Selection]] — preservation of slight, useful, heritable variations through competition.
- [[Struggle-for-Existence]] — the severe competition among organisms that follows from over-reproduction.
- [[Adaptation]] — fit between organism and its infinitely complex conditions of life.
- [[Descent-with-Modification]] — the branching "Tree of Life" from common ancestors.
- [[Divergence-of-Character]] — how selection pushes descendants to fill different places in nature's economy.

## Extracted claims
- Any variation, however slight, that is profitable to an individual in its complex relations to nature tends to be preserved and inherited; Darwin names this principle "Natural Selection" by analogy to man's power of selection. ^[raw/books/darwin-origin-of-species/book.md:2107-2123]
- Because far more individuals are produced than can survive, there must in every case be a struggle for existence — the doctrine of Malthus applied with manifold force to the whole animal and vegetable kingdoms. ^[raw/books/darwin-origin-of-species/book.md:2176-2185]
- Where a place in nature's economy is not perfectly occupied, natural selection will always tend to preserve the individuals varying in the right direction so as to better fill the unoccupied place. ^[raw/books/darwin-origin-of-species/book.md:3305-3311]
- The affinities of all beings form a "great Tree of Life," whose dead and broken branches fill the earth's crust and whose living ramifications cover its surface — an image of descent from common ancestors. ^[raw/books/darwin-origin-of-species/book.md:4131-4135]
- Because natural selection works solely by and for the good of each being, all corporeal and mental endowments will tend to progress toward perfection. ^[raw/books/darwin-origin-of-species/book.md:14585-14592]

## Contrasted with
- [[The-Republic-Plato]] — Plato posits eternal, unchanging Forms and fixed natural kinds, opposite to Darwin's mutable, historically contingent species.
- [[Nicomachean-Ethics-Aristotle]] — Aristotle's teleology gives each organism a fixed essence and purpose, whereas Darwin explains apparent design without a designer or final cause.
- [[Meditations-Marcus-Aurelius]] — the Stoic providential, rationally ordered cosmos contrasts with Darwin's blind, undirected selection.

## See also

- [[Leviathan-Hobbes]] — the "struggle for existence" echoes Hobbes's competitive state of nature and war of all against all.
- [[On-War-Clausewitz]] — competition, attrition, and survival under relentless pressure as an organizing frame.
