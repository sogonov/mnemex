# Is virtue an instrument of power, or an end in itself?

> The corpus splits sharply: Machiavelli treats virtue as a mask worn for
> advantage, while Franklin and Marcus Aurelius treat it as the goal itself.

## Question

When these authors praise "virtue," do they mean a tool for getting results, or
a good worth pursuing for its own sake?

## Short answer

Machiavelli is instrumental: a prince need not *have* the virtues, only *appear*
to, and should abandon them when necessity demands. Franklin is developmental —
virtue is a lifelong project pursued and measured for its own sake — and Marcus
Aurelius is intrinsic — virtue is living according to reason, the only true good.

## Detailed answer

According to [[The-Prince-Machiavelli]], appearing virtuous is useful while being
virtuous can be ruinous; virtue is subordinate to the state. This contrasts with
[[Autobiography-Franklin]], where virtue is the explicit end — tracked daily in a
little book — and with [[Meditations-Marcus-Aurelius]], for whom virtue is simply
right reason, independent of any external payoff.
